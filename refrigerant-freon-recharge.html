Critical Protection

Why Operating Low on Refrigerant Destroys Your AC

Many Naples homeowners don't realize that AC refrigerant does more than absorb heat—it also acts as the primary cooling fluid for the hermetic compressor motor itself.

When refrigerant charge drops, the compressor motor temperature spikes past 250°F. The compressor oil breaks down into corrosive acid, leading to mechanical seizure and complete system burnout costing thousands of dollars to replace.

Additionally, low suction pressure causes the indoor evaporator coil to drop below freezing, encasing your air handler in a solid block of ice that blocks airflow and floods ceilings when it melts.

Common Inquiries

Frequently Asked Questions About AC Recharge

What are the common signs my AC needs a refrigerant recharge in Naples?

The most common symptoms include vents blowing warm or lukewarm air, ice forming along the refrigerant copper lines or evaporator coil, hissing or bubbling noises near the outdoor unit, AC running non-stop without reaching thermostat setting, and a sudden spike in your monthly FPL electric bill.

Can you just add Freon to my air conditioner without fixing the leak?

Air conditioning systems are closed hermetic circuits that never consume or burn refrigerant. If your AC is low on Freon, it has an active leak. Simply topping off refrigerant without locating and sealing the leak violates EPA regulations and will result in the new refrigerant leaking out again within days or weeks.

What is the difference between R-22 Freon and modern R-410A refrigerant?

R-22 (traditional Freon) is an ozone-depleting hydrochlorofluorocarbon that was phased out under the Clean Air Act. Modern systems use R-410A (Puron), which operates at higher pressures and is environmentally safer. Our technicians service both legacy R-22 and current R-410A systems.

How does coastal salt air cause refrigerant leaks in Naples homes?

Naples' salty sea breezes accelerate formicary corrosion on copper tubing and aluminum condenser fins. Over time, microscopic pinhole leaks develop in the U-bends and service valves. We use electronic sniffers and nitrogen bubble tests to locate these tiny coastal leaks.
